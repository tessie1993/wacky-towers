Status: study draft for the creative director. Nothing here is approved.
Author: level design lead (study pass). Date: 2026-10-14.

## 0. How this was done

- I looked at every panel on mockups 05-20, 32-37, the four party boards, the biome screens board and the two biome sheets. I also opened the style minigame boards (they hold Fort Frenzy, Fort Blast and Kraken Blockade).
- I could not crop or enlarge. This session has no shell, so no PIL. All notes come from full-board views. Small HUD text may be misread. See the questions at the end.
- Logos on the boards ("TETRO PARTY", "WACKY PARTY", "PARTY QUEST") are not copied. Panel names are quoted only to point at the panel.
- `party_biome_islands_ref.jpg` shows Nintendo characters. I used it for layout only (kingdom island, lava keep with boss statue and sentries, neon glass board). No IP is carried into any idea.
- Style board file numbers do not match index.md. I name panels by their content, not their file number.

## 1. The big lessons in one table

| Lesson | What the boards do |
|---|---|
| The board is a prop inside a place | Deck, cliff top, conveyor, cake plate, gear, iceberg, clam shell, space-station slab. Never a floating grid on a flat backdrop. |
| Something acts | Kraken, boulder, lava, wind cloud, avalanche, cannon fire, black hole, laser drones, giant. The threat is always visible and in the same frame as the board. |
| Cast on stage | 2 to 4 small characters stand on or beside the board and do the verb (carry, throw, crank, aim). |
| One hero meter | One tall thermometer, one top bar, or two bars. Themed icon at the base (flame, drop, heart, gear). Named in the setting's own words. |
| Next piece is a themed card | Next Block, Next Ammo, Next Ramp, Next Conduit, Next Item. The card art is the piece in that biome's material. |
| Goal is on the screen | A caption ("BRIDGE THE GAP!", "FORTRESS SMASH!"), a trophy, an idol, a flag, a bomb. You know the win before you read text. |
| Camera | Almost always a high three-quarter isometric view. The board is centre, lit brightest, with the most contrast. Edges fall to dark or blur. |

### 3.2 What the defence panels teach

1. The attacker is huge and sits at the edge of the frame. The wall sits between them and you. The wall is the brightest thing.
2. Walls show damage by looking broken: missing bricks, cracks, a smoking hole. Damage is readable without a number.
3. A defence meter is named for the thing: Hull, Fort, Egg Temperature, Grid Heat. It is never "HP".
4. The attack has a telegraph: an arc preview, a tentacle rising, a meteor with a flame tail. This is the pacing tool for waves.
5. Rebuild needs a repair verb. Hammer, trowel, cannon, wand, carry.
6. Two panels (Fort Frenzy, Fort Blast) mix attack and defence. For our game, keep one side. Attack is a reward beat, not the core.

### 3.3 Defence scenario family for falling blocks

Our pieces fall. They land where the player steers them. So the wall is the pile.

| Pattern | Rule | Pressure | Win | Panel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Soak wall | A wave hits the wall column. Each hit removes the top cube or cracks a face. | Wave telegraph, then hit | Last N waves | Boss Battle Build, Kraken Blockade |
| Core shield | A core sits in the centre. Falling blocks must ring it, one layer at a time. Attacks come from set sides. | Open sides are weak | Core survives N waves | Prehistoric Nest, Cyber Defender |
| Plug the breach | A crack opens in the wall. Fill it with a matching piece before the next hit. | Timer on the crack | Breaches stay closed | Fort Blast |
| Last N waves | Waves are numbered and the wall must stand. | Rising wave size | Wave N ends | Kraken Siege |
| Moving beam | A beam sweeps the board. Place a block in its path to take the hit. A hot block dims for a while. | Heat per block | Hold until power-down | Nebula Laser Hack |
| Cargo stack | Keep a cargo pile under cover during a storm. | Wind side and rain | Reach harbour | Kraken Keep |
| Lure and shield | A guest creature walks a path. Walls guide it to the exit. | Enemy sees gaps | Guest gets home | Temple Roller, Dino Jungle |
| Rebuild between waves | A calm beat between waves. The wall is repaired with a larger piece. | Short timer | Fort at full | Fort Blast |

Rules to keep it kid-safe: creatures bonk, splash, bounce or knock things over. Nobody is hurt. Damage is cracks, dust, cargo overboard, eggs that wobble.

## 6. HUD and staging lessons

| Our game lacks | The boards show | Fix to test |
|---|---|---|
| A hero meter named in the biome's words | "Hull Integrity", "Egg Temperature", "Topple Risk" | One meter per level with its own name and icon base. |
| Meter art | Tall thermometer with a themed icon at the base | Use an icon per biome: flame, drop, gear, heart, star. |
| Next card in the piece's material | Next Ammo, Next Ramp, Next Conduit | Card art in biome material. |
| Goal caption | "GOAL: BRIDGE THE GAP!" parchment | One-line goal at level start. Keep it short. |
| Visible attacker | Kraken, giant, boulder | Place it in the same view as the board. |
| Telegraph | Arc preview, tentacle rising | Every hit has a 1 to 2 second cue. |
| Diegetic HUD | Quantum Spire screens on the slab | Put the next card on a prop (bolted screen, crate, chalk board). |
| Guest beside the board | Biome screens, Fruit Stack | One large friendly guest per level that reacts. |
| Board shape not square | Ring, loop, causeway, plate, clam | Pick a shape per biome. |
| Material twist | Ice vs lava cubes, bubble cubes | One rule per material. |
| Rival or ally on the board | Mummy on same stack, divers | Cast climbs the stack. |
| Progress pins | Meter with portrait pins, hub path pins | Pins for cast on the main meter. |
| Hub scaffold | Wooden tower with shelves | Use shelves in meadow. |

Composition and colour tricks that help the board read:

- The board is the brightest and most saturated region. The surround is darker, cooler or blurred.
- Warm threat against cool board, or the reverse (purple kraken on teal sea, orange lava on grey rock).
- One accent colour for the goal (gold idol, red bomb, checkered flag).
- Lines lead in: belts, rails, bridges, beams, spirals all point at the board.
- Tall silhouettes at the edge (towers, volcano, giant) frame the board and show scale.
- Glow tells state: orange cracks, blue path tiles, red hit flash.
