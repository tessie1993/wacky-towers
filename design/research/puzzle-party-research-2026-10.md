# Puzzle & Party Genre Research — More Content for Wacky Towers

> **Status**: Research (input for `/brainstorm`, `/design-system`, `/quick-design`)
> **Author**: game-design researcher (agent) for Tessa
> **Date**: 2026-10-10
> **Scope**: Ideas for more puzzles, scenes, varied levels and new modes, mapped onto what the code already does.
> **Reads**: `design/gdd/game-concept.md`, `campaign-structure.md`, `level-goals-fail-states.md`, `mechanics-wave3.md`, `party-minigames-wave2.md`, `tournament-minigames.md`, `src/mechanics/README.md`, `src/mechanics/goal/*.gd`, and the `goal`/`fixed_list` fields of all 143 level JSONs in `src/levels/`.

## 0. Where the content stands now (baseline)

The research only matters next to what is already built, so here is the current state:

| Thing | What exists | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Goal types used in levels | `clear_n` 177, `shape` 36, `height` 21, `clear` 21, `survive` 12, `bonk_boss` 5, `wind_keys` 4, `rescue_all` 2, `fill_shape` 2, `dig_rescue` 2, `bake_oven` 1 (counts include the duplicated `metadata.design_spec` copies) | About **70 % of goals are "clear N layers"**. Variety lives mostly in rules (twists/mechanics), not in what the player is *asked to do*. |
| Goal plugins in `src/mechanics/goal/` | `clear_n`, `height`, `shape`, `colour_target`, `survive`, `endless`, `bonk_boss`, `dig_rescue`, `rescue_all`, `wind_keys` | Plus top-out slots `rescue`, `trim`, `lose`, `budget_top_out`, `piece_budget_top_out`, `out_of_pieces_top_out`. The piece-budget and out-of-pieces slots already make a real puzzle mode possible. |
| Fixed-piece puzzle levels (`pieces.fixed_list` non-empty) | **10 of 143** (meadow/candy/lava/underwater bonus, lava_h3, cave_07, and the `_06` tier of forest/neon/clockwork/celestial) | Rule 15 asks for 1–2 per biome, so this is on target. But a handcrafted puzzle *track* (Tetris DS Puzzle, Tricky Towers Puzzle trials) does not exist yet. |
| Rule behaviours | ~45 core + 15 wave-3 (`magnet_pull` … `golden_row`) | A big toolbox. Almost every archetype below can be built from existing rules plus at most one new goal or param. |
| Party minigames | MG1–MG36 on `WtMinigameSession`/`WtMinigameRound` (MG5 is a native Jolt scene) | All of them are **free-for-all**, with sends between players. The repo has no **1v3, 2v2, co-op or duel** formats, and these are the main thing that makes Mario Party's rounds feel different from each other. |
| Scenes | `campaign_map`, `title_screen`, `result_panel`, `minigame_ui`, galleries (cast/shape/party art), `wt_physics_challenge`, `wt_crane_tower`, `wordless_story`, biome stages | There is no hub, puzzle-select, daily, trophy, lobby or podium scene. |

**Headline:** the cheapest way to get more variety is not more rules. It is (a) **more kinds of goal and constraint** on the existing rules, (b) **new team formats** for the existing 36 minigames, and (c) **structures** (puzzle gallery, daily, mission ladder) that reuse levels the game already has.

## 1. Findings by theme

### 1.1 Fixed-piece puzzles are their own pleasure, and every falling-block series ships them

- **Tricky Towers** has 50 single-player trials in three types. **Race** (reach a finish line before time runs out), **Survival** (three lives and a fixed brick count, and you must place every brick), and **Puzzle** (place a fixed number of bricks under a laser line, and any brick above the line or dropped fails the trial). Reviewers call the trials "preset puzzles" that train skills for multiplayer. The same three types come back as multiplayer modes, each in easy/normal/special variants. Sources: [Giant Bomb wiki](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Tricky_Towers), [Xbox Addict review](https://xboxaddict.com/Staff-Review/14006/Tricky-Towers.html), [Indie Ranger review](https://indieranger.com/tricky-towers-review).
  - *Transfer:* the **laser-line puzzle** ("use every piece and stay under line H") maps directly onto `fixed_list` + `goal: none/clear` + `budget_top_out`. The **"every brick placed" survival** is our `out_of_pieces` win condition.
- **Tetris DS** has three distinct solo structures. **Mission** gives one objective at a time ("clear N lines with one piece", "clear under fast drop"), and a time-attack ladder chains 10 missions. **Puzzle** asks you to clear the board with a set number of pieces, with up to 200 puzzles. **Push** is a tug-of-war versus where multi-line clears shove a shared block toward the opponent. Source: [Wikipedia: Tetris DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_DS), [Tetris Wiki](https://tetris.wiki/Tetris_DS).
  - *Transfer:* the **Mission Ladder** (§3.2) and the **Push** duel (§2 archetype 23).
- **Tetris Party / Deluxe / Axis** mode list. **Shadow**: fill a design with pieces, with up to 10 skips per stage. **Field Climber / Tower Climber**: build stairs for a little character who gets crushed if a piece lands on him. **Stage Racer**: steer one piece down an obstacle course. **Bombliss**: bombs inside pieces explode on clear. Sources: [Tetris Wiki: Tetris Party](https://tetris.wiki/Tetris_Party), [Wikipedia: Tetris Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris:_Axis).
  - *Transfer:* **Shadow + skips** is our `shape` goal with a "skip" budget. **Field Climber** is a critter-escort level built on our mascot and `mascot_catch` vocabulary. **Stage Racer** is a single-piece maze (new, but small). **Bombliss** is close to `ember`, `items` Bomb and `anvil_drop`.
- **Puyo Puyo Tetris** shows that mode remixes give a lot of variety for little cost. **Swap** alternates rule sets every 20–30 s. **Fusion** puts both piece types on one board. **Big Bang** is a race to clear identical pre-built boards. **Party** adds power-ups. **Adventure** is 10 acts of 10 stages that alternate CPU battles and solo challenges. Sources: [tetris.com: PPT modes explained](https://tetris.com/article/36/puyo-puyo-tetris-modes-explained), [Wikipedia: PPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puyo_Puyo_Tetris).
  - *Transfer:* **Swap** is a "stage swap" timer that cycles rule bundles. **Big Bang** is a seeded identical pre-built board race (`set_board` exists on `WtMinigameSession`). Adventure's 10×10 shape matches our campaign exactly. Interleaving **rival battles** into campaign tiers is a cheap variety lever.
- **Tetris Effect: Connected.** **Connected** mode has three players link boards against an AI boss, and the boards periodically merge into one shared board where players take turns. **Zone Battle** is a duel with a time-stop. Sources: [Destructoid review](https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-tetris-effect-connected/), [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/?p=145727).
  - *Transfer:* a **Co-op Tower** where 2–4 players take turns placing on one shared board against a boss (§3.4). Our `stopwatch` rule is already a Zone-like freeze.
- **Tetris 99**: targeting strategies (KOs / Badges / Attackers / Random) and a bonus when you are attacked by several players at once. Sources: [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=235066), [Tetris Wiki](https://tetris.wiki/Tetris_99), [Digital Trends](https://qa2.www.digitaltrends.com/?p=2293916).
  - *Transfer:* our send routing (`_target()` in `WtMinigameSession`) could expose a **target mode** choice. "Attacked by many → stronger sends" fits the *Comeback Energy* pillar.
- **3D falling-block readability (Blockout).** Pieces are shown as wireframes before landing, and grid lines run up the pit walls. The parts of a piece that stick out into the next layer get a hotter colour. Sources: [Sega Retro](https://segaretro.org/705501), [Legacy of Games](https://legacyofgames.com/2026/04/16/blockout/).
  - *Transfer:* "overhang colour" and wall grid lines make **minimum-moves and silhouette puzzles** fair in 3D. Each puzzle archetype should check the camera and readability in its first level.

### 1.2 How puzzle levels are constructed (teach → test → twist)

- **Baba Is You.** Arvi Teikari designs backwards: he starts from the solution, then adds restrictions that close every easier route. Players describe each level as *obvious approach → it breaks → twist that reframes → reward*. A Steam thread warns that if a twist relies on an idea the game never showed, players feel cheated. Sources: [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-baba-is-you/), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/736260/discussions/0/1647665620937292845), [GMTK transcript](https://amara.org/subtitles/v4T6ECRKBPBx/en/1/download/How%20Baba%20Is%20You%20Works%20%20GMTK%20Most%20Innovative%202019.en.txt).
  - *Transfer:* author each Puzzle Gallery level **solution-first**: place the intended final tower, then derive the `fixed_list` from it. Then add blockers (pre-placed cubes, `rusty_hinge` rotation caps, `fog`) until shortcuts fail. Every twist must have been shown, alone, earlier in the gallery.
- **Stephen's Sausage Roll** gets years of puzzles out of one fixed tiny rule set. Difficulty comes from more interacting objects (a late level has 8 sausages where most have 2). Reviewers credit "deliberate friction" that stops accidental solves. Sources: [Thinky Games 10-year retrospective](https://thinkygames.com/features/10-years-of-grilling-stephens-sausage-roll-remains-one-of-the-most-influential-puzzle-games-ever-created/), [Quarter to Three forum](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/stephen-lavelle-s-sausage-roll/78477), [Slant review](https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/stephens-sausage-roll/).
  - *Transfer:* the Puzzle Gallery should be allowed to use **no twists**, only board size, pre-placed cubes and piece list. The difficulty lever is *how many pieces must interlock*, not how many rules are active.
- **Portal** teaches with a safe chamber first, then raises the challenge, then combines mechanics, with no on-screen tutorial text. Sources: [Combine OverWiki: Portal developer commentary](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Developer_commentary/Portal), [Giant Bomb: Thinking With Portals](https://giantbomb.com/users/3455/articles/thinking-with-portals), [TweakTown review](https://www.tweaktown.com/gaming/2575/portal_pc_review/index.html).
  - *Transfer:* this matches campaign rule 13 (teach 1–2 → twist 3–8 → remix 9 → finale 10). The same arc should run *inside* a Puzzle Gallery wing: about 8 puzzles per wing on one theme.
- **Sokoban.** Deadlocks (a crate in a corner, two crates locked against a wall) create the tension. Good solvers plan the order backwards. Sources: [Dinogame: Mastering Sokoban](https://dinogame.gg/blog/mastering-sokoban/), [JSoko deadlocks](https://jsokoapplet.sourceforge.io/sokoban/deadlocks.html).
  - *Transfer:* in a fixed-list puzzle, a **covered hole is the deadlock**. Design puzzles where one tempting early placement makes a hole that cannot be undone. Offer **undo** (one step back) in Puzzle Gallery only.
- **Picross / Nonograms.** A good puzzle has **exactly one** solution, can be solved by line logic alone without guessing, and its clue totals must agree. Sources: [Wikipedia: Nonogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonogram), [puzzles.wiki](https://www.puzzles.wiki/wiki/Nonogram).
  - *Transfer:* a **"Block-o-gram"** archetype. Row and column counts are printed on the frame walls, and the player builds the hidden 3D figure (or a 2D side face of it). The `shape` goal already checks cell targets, so the only new part is a clue display. Run a uniqueness check in the level validator.
- **Professor Layton** varies puzzle *types* constantly (visual, maths, logic). Each puzzle shows a value that drops about 10 % per wrong answer. There are three hint tiers, each paid with scarce **hint coins** found by exploring, and a later "super hint" costs two. Sources: [Wikipedia: Curious Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Layton_and_the_Curious_Village), [StrategyWiki: Hint Coins](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Professor_Layton_and_the_Curious_Village/Hint_Coins).
  - *Transfer:* the **Hint Acorn** economy for Puzzle Gallery. `mascot_hint` and `WtPerfectFit.suggestions()` already produce a legal suggested placement. Tier 1 highlights the next piece's region, tier 2 shows the orientation, tier 3 shows the exact ghost.
- **Monument Valley.** Each level changes its rules. Rotating or sliding structures makes impossible paths line up. Sources: [GDC 2014 via Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/058349/r/), [Mechanics of Magic critical play](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2024/05/12/monument-valley-critical-play-puzzles/).
  - *Transfer:* a **"from this angle"** archetype. The goal silhouette is only correct from one fixed camera angle, like a perspective illusion. It uses `camera-rotate-view` with the `shape` goal evaluated on a projection.
- **Captain Toad.** Levels are cube dioramas that you solve by rotating the camera. Each level has 3 gems, plus a **secret bonus objective that only appears after the first clear** (no damage, find the gold mushroom, collect N coins). Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Toad:_Treasure_Tracker), [Engadget review](https://www.engadget.com/2014-12-01-captain-toad-treasure-tracker-review-locked-and-toaded.html).
  - *Transfer:* a **"Second Visit" bonus objective** on every campaign level, unlocked after the first finish. This multiplies replay content at almost zero authoring cost (§3.3). `angle_gem` already gives camera-revealed secrets.

### 1.3 Goal variety and obstacle cadence (mobile puzzle sagas)

- **Candy Crush.** The core level types are score-in-moves, jelly (clear every marked cell), ingredients (bring objects to the bottom), timed, orders (collect N of X) and mixed. New obstacles arrive regularly: spreading chocolate, licorice, marmalade, countdown bombs. Sources: [TV Tropes: Candy Crush](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/CandyCrush), fan wikis (unverified).
  - *Transfer:* we have the equivalents of jelly (`shape`), ingredients (`dig_rescue`/`rescue_all`), timed (`survive`) and score. We **lack orders** ("clear 3 layers containing gold", "pop 10 red cubes"), and **mixed goals** (two goals at once, as in Candy's "mixed" levels). Spreading obstacles already exist (`goo_spread`, `pest`).
- **Two Dots.** Goals are shown upfront with a move cap. Anchors sink to the bottom when you clear what is under them. Ice cracks over repeated clears. A **new themed obstacle about every 20 levels**. Sources: [Wikipedia: Two Dots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Dots_(video_game)), [Gamezebo review](https://www.gamezebo.com/reviews/twodots-review-brother-can-you-spare-a-dot/).
  - *Transfer:* a **move cap** (piece budget, which exists as `piece_budget_top_out`) is the casual mobile's main tension lever, and our levels barely use it. **Anchor** is a cube that wants to reach the floor, the inverse of `dig_rescue`.
- **Threes.** Vollmer wanted a game that "always provides you with a worthy challenge you can take on at your own pace". The team spent half of 14 months iterating the one core idea and gave tiles personalities. Sources: [Wikipedia: Threes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threes), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-01-30-addictive-ios-puzzling-comes-in-threes.html).
  - *Transfer:* the no-timer **"Zen puzzle"** variant (gravity off until you drop), so puzzles can be played at your own pace. Give the blocks faces.
- **Lumines.** "Skins" pair song, colours and background. A **timeline** sweeps in time with the music and clears marked squares. Voyage mode alternates fast and slow skins like an album instead of ramping linearly. Sources: [Tetris Wiki news: Lumines](https://news.tetris.wiki/Lumines), [Pocket Gamer review](https://www.pocketgamer.com/lumines-electronic-symphony/review/).
  - *Transfer:* a **rhythm boss** where telegraphs and clears are paced to the beat (§2 archetype 19). Biome order should alternate intensity, like an album, and not climb steadily.
- **Wordle.** One shared puzzle per day, a spoiler-free emoji share grid, a streak, and a session of a few minutes. Sources: [Dinogame: Wordle review](https://dinogame.gg/blog/wordle-review/), [Internet Matters](https://www.internetmatters.org/hub/esafety-news/what-is-the-wordle-game/).
  - *Transfer:* the **Daily Tower** (§3.3) with a seeded `fixed_list` from the date, and a share card showing a coloured 4×4 "heat grid" of your tower without the solution.

### 1.4 Party structure and round formats

- **Mario Party categories.** **4-Player** free-for-all. **1-vs-3** (one player against a team of three; MP9/10 "1-vs-Rivals"). **2-vs-2** teams. **Battle**: everyone stakes coins and the pot splits 70/25/5. **Duel**: the winner takes coins or stars from the loser. Plus solo Challenge, a Last/Bowser minigame, and Item/Rare categories. Sources: [MarioWiki: MP8 minigames](https://www.mariowiki.com/List_of_Mario_Party_8_minigames), [MarioWiki: Battle minigame](https://www.mariowiki.com/Battle_minigame), [MarioWiki: MP3 list](https://www.mariowiki.com/List_of_Mario_Party_3_minigames).
  - *Transfer:* add **team format** as a template field on our minigames (`format: ffa | 1v3 | 2v2 | coop | duel | battle`). Many existing MGs work as 2v2 (team score = sum) or 1v3 (the solo player gets the send effect on all three) without new rules code.
- **Super Mario Party Jamboree.** **Bowser Kaboom Squad**: 8-player co-op that collects bombs in timed sections and fires them at Bowser, with competitive minigames in between for boosts, while Bowser scales up. **Koopathlon**: a 20-player race where minigame coins move you around a lap track. Sources: [Inverse preview](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/super-mario-party-jamboree-preview-new-game-modes-battle-royale), [Nintendo Life guide](https://nintendolife.com/guides/super-mario-party-jamboree-multiplayer-requirements-local-and-online-guide), [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2024/10/27/massively-on-the-go-hands-on-with-super-mario-party-jamborees-online-modes/).
  - *Transfer:* a **co-op boss tournament** variant ("Tower vs. Giant"). Players win minigames to earn bricks for a *shared* tower that must outgrow the boss's wrecking ball.
- **WarioWare microgames** each have **one verb** with an exclamation mark ("Dodge!"), last about **4 seconds (8 beats)**, and the instruction itself uses part of that time. Speed rises between rounds, and a boss game comes every N. Sources: [Wikipedia: WarioWare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WarioWare), [MarioWiki: Microgames](https://www.mariowiki.com/Microgames), [Game Studies: Gingold](https://gamestudies.org/0501/gingold).
  - *Transfer:* **Block Blitz** (§3.5). Many of our 36 minigame verbs (rotate to fit, spot the twin, tap the pop-up, time the drop) compress to 4-second microgames on the same session code.
- **Ultimate Chicken Horse.** Each round, players pick from a random "party box" of blocks, place them into a shared level, then all race it. You score only if you finish **and** someone else fails, so a trap you place must be beatable by you. Sources: [Nintendo eShop](https://www.nintendo.com/store/products/ultimate-chicken-horse-switch/), [MobyGames](https://mobygames.com/game/84580/ultimate-chicken-horse), [design class write-up](https://mycours.es/gamedesign2017/2017/10/27/homeplay-ultimate-chicken-horse-2/).
  - *Transfer:* **Build-then-Play** (§3.6). Each player picks an obstacle or twist card for the shared next round. The "you must survive it too" rule keeps sabotage self-limiting.
- **Pico Park.** A stage clears only when a key is fetched and **every** player reaches the door. Each of its 48 levels has a gimmick built for multiplayer, and new mechanics keep arriving so groups have to keep negotiating. Sources: [Steam: PICO PARK](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1509960/PICO_PARK/), [Nintendo eShop](https://www.nintendo.com/es-cl/store/products/pico-park-switch/).
  - *Transfer:* **co-op "all hands" puzzles**. Each player controls one piece colour or one axis, and the solution needs everyone.
- **Overcooked.** Playtesters settled into efficient routines, so the designers added layout disruptions (moving trucks, splitting islands) that force talking and changing roles. Sources: [Superjump: How design creates teamwork](https://superjumpmagazine.com/overcooked-how-design-creates-teamwork), [PC Gamer review](https://pcgamer.com/overcooked-review).
  - *Transfer:* co-op boards should **change mid-round** (`turntable`, `mill_belt`, `flip`, `thin_ice` already do this), so a shared tower cannot be solved by one player taking charge.
- **Fall Guys.** Round types are Race (about two-thirds qualify), Survival, Logic (Perfect Match: memorise fruit tiles), Team, Hunt and Final. The Final is always round 5, uses an existing category, and whoever wins it wins the show. Sources: [Wikipedia: Fall Guys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Guys), [PlayStation Competition Center](https://compete.playstation.com/en-us/news/176475), [Red Bull guide](https://www.redbull.com/ie-en/how-to-win-fall-guys-ultimate-knockout-guide).
  - *Transfer:* an **elimination "Gauntlet"** tournament format. Rounds qualify only the top N, and a guaranteed "Final" round type closes the show. This adds drama to our majority-wins format.
- **Jackbox.** Each phone is a private screen, so games can hand out hidden roles (Push the Button). Spectators join as an Audience that votes. Sources: [Built In: Jackbox design principles](https://builtin.com/media-gaming/jackbox-games-design-party-pack), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id971879034).
  - *Transfer:* on mobile multiplayer each device can hold **secret info**: a hidden saboteur in co-op, or a private target silhouette. A spectator who has been knocked out can **vote on the next twist**. Today our ghost players "may only send"; voting is a gentler alternative.
