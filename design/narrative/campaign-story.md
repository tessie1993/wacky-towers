# Wacky Towers campaign story: The Twelve Keystones

Author: studio narrative content lead. Date: 2026-10-14. Status: phase 2 draft for the 9.7 narrative review (rubric N1-N6).
Inputs: `design/levels/plans/campaign-vision.md` (pillars, arc, U1-U16, briefs), `campaign-redo-plan.md`, `progression.md`,
`minigames.md`, `level-identity-spec.md` (including the later "no mundane" user rules), `carnival.md`, `tumble-fair.md`,
`design/art/art-bible.md`, boards 01, 02 and 07 (layout only), and `design/narrative/research.md` (rules R1-R12).
Decisions in force: 168 levels; a FRESH story that keeps only the cast; the user's later rules (no "clear N layers",
surprise endings, a moment in every level); **real tension in Lava and Neon** (darker, a sense of loss, kid-safe,
no on-screen harm); the story ends with the Carnival boss plus a bonus celebration, and **h1-h3 are an optional epilogue,
"the villain strikes back"**. Nothing here is borrowed from the old story, old level names or story-v2.

## 1. The story

**In two sentences.** The Wizard built the tallest tower in the sky and topped it with the twelve Keystones that hold the
islands up, but he shooed away Huffle, the sky's giant baby cloud, who only wanted to know if it was hers, so she huffed and
the tower tumbled and the Keystones flew off. Dame Ada, Bram, Sif and you, the steady-handed Builder, follow the Keystones
island to island, rebuilding each island's own tower so its Keystone will stay put, and learn that the "monster" is a lonely
baby who wanted someone to build with.

**The wacky twist.** The villain is a toddler in a cloud blanket. The Wizard's flaw (kind, forgetful, vain) caused it, and he
told everyone he had "just sneezed". The last tower is not a monument: it is Huffle's birthday Ferris wheel, promised ages ago.

**Stakes.** Each island sags, drifts or goes odd without its Keystone. In Neon the sag becomes visible on the islands we already
mended, so the loss is felt. If all twelve are not home, the sky stays wobbly for good.

**World rule: honest stacks hold.** Blocks remember how they were placed. A tower built from the island's own problem upward
holds its Keystone; a sloppy pile only wobbles. A finished layer settles with a warm hum; a win lights the island and the Tether
(a thin golden thread from each Keystone to the next) grows brighter. A toppled stack is never a disaster: the blocks giggle
back into their box and try again. (Ludonarrative harmony: the cleared layer *is* the island healing.)

**Why the player stacks.** The Wizard's wand only *drops* blocks (they tumble from his hat brim, which is why pieces fall and
the next piece waits at the brim). His showy hands cannot place them true. You can. You never swing a sword.

**Why the order matters.** Huffle's huff blew the Keystones outward in a spiral, nearest first. Each Keystone you set hums one
more note of the **Sky Lullaby** and its Tether points to the next island. The order is Meadow to Carnival because that is the
order the wind dropped them (Pirate Cove, flung farthest out to sea, is eleventh), and because Huffle's own lullaby is the last note.

**Why it is not an errand quest.** Every chapter has its own villain with a stacking problem, a local ally and a feeling. The
Keystone is a payoff, never the plot of a level.

### 2.4 Huffle (the force)

A huge, round, lilac baby cloud in a blanket of cloud, big wet eyes, puffed cheeks (art bible: furious wind cloud softened to a
sulking toddler). She loves knocking blocks over and hearing them clatter. She hoards shiny blocks, so she still holds
**Keystone twelve**. She appears as a shadow from chapter 1 (a distant huff, a lilac swirl at the edge of each island's sky) and
is met in chapter 11.

## 3. Text, tone and hand-off rules

- **In-level moments are wordless.** No dialogue and no text over the board. Wizard, heroes and allies react with poses and sound.
- **Captions** are 12 words or fewer, on a purple-wood or gold plaque over a painted panel (never parchment or paper), live UI,
  never baked into art. The 12 chapter captions plus the prologue and epilogue captions carry the whole arc.
- **Names** are 1-4 words with at most one pun. None equals or paraphrases a reference-board title or a `minigames.md` Naming fix.
  Not used here: "Tumble Fair" (a board title; the Carnival is **Huffle's Birthday Fair**), Molten Guardian, Kraken Blockade,
  Fort Blast, Tumbling Towers, Bridge Bashers, Maze Heist, Cosmic Gateway, Grand Finale, Story Victory.
- **Real tension (user decision).** Lava and Neon carry real loss and fear (a hero lost in smoke, the sky sagging, an ally dimming)
  before the comeback. Never on-screen harm, never cruelty; every low beat resolves inside its chapter or the next.
- **Beat table columns.** `ID`, `Beat` (title), `What happens` (2 sentences: the first is the opening event in the first 10
  seconds, the second is the story objective and the payoff), `Objective` (8 words or fewer, a story goal, never "clear N
  layers"), `Place` (set piece and time of day), `Who`, `Ending` (`plain`, `twist`, `reveal`, `guest`, `secret` or `story` plus
  the surprise in 12 words or fewer). At least 4 per chapter are not plain and no pattern appears more than twice (U16).
- **Mechanics are the systems designer's.** Beats name the situation and the building verb; they do not pick rules. Each
  chapter lists the biome's existing mechanics (from `progression.md`) only as hooks for phase 3.
- **Per-beat YAML files** (`design/narrative/beats/<biome>.md`) are derived from these tables in the next step. They were not part of this
  task's authorised outputs, so they are not written here. Opening event = sentence 1 of each row.
