# Wacky Towers campaign story: The Twelve Keystones

Author: studio narrative content lead. Date: 2026-10-14. Status: phase 2, revision 2 (after narrative review r1, `production/qa/quality-review/2026-10-14-narrative-r1.md`).
Inputs: `campaign-vision.md` (pillars, arc, U1-U17, rubric N), `scene-vocabulary.md`, `exemplars.md`, `minigames.md`, `minigame-party-rules.md`,
`reference-study-defence.md`, `reference-study-mockups.md`, `level-identity-spec.md`, `carnival.md`, `tumble-fair.md`, `art-bible.md`, boards 01, 02, 07
(layout only) and `design/narrative/research.md` (rules R1-R12).
Decisions in force: 168 levels; a FRESH story that keeps only the cast; no counter goals; surprise endings; a moment in every level; real tension in Lava and
Neon (kid-safe, no on-screen harm); Pirate Cove is 11th; the story ends with the Carnival boss plus a bonus celebration and h1-h3 are an optional epilogue,
"the villain strikes back". **New user rulings (applied in this revision):** (A) "Minigames are the level": every level is a minigame built on 1 or 2 mechanics,
so every beat carries a verb and a hook. (B) "All scenes were square grids": every level has its own board footprint, none repeated inside a biome.

## 1. The story

**In two sentences.** The Wizard's tallest tower fell when Huffle, a lonely baby cloud, huffed, scattering the twelve Keystones that hold the islands up. Ada, Bram,
Sif and you stack each island's own tower to catch its Keystone, learning she only wanted a playmate.

**The wacky twist.** The villain is a toddler in a cloud blanket. The Wizard's flaw (kind, forgetful, vain) caused it, and he told everyone he had "just sneezed".
The last tower is not a monument: it is Huffle's birthday Ferris wheel, promised long ago. The promise is revealed once, in chapter 12 (carnival_08). Earlier
hints (a blueprint of something round, a wheel-shaped star ring, a map with an X) are unexplained props and never spell it out.

**Stakes.** Each island sags, drifts or goes odd without its Keystone. In Neon the sag shows on the islands we already mended and is played (neon_08).

**World rule: honest stacks hold.** Blocks remember how they were placed: a tidy block hums and keeps its glow, a careless one wobbles and giggles (shown in
meadow_01). A tower built from the island's own problem upward holds its Keystone; a sloppy pile only wobbles. A win lights the island and the Tether (a golden
thread from each Keystone to the next). A toppled stack is never a disaster: the blocks giggle back into their box. The cleared layer is the island healing.

**Why the player stacks.** The Wizard's wand only drops blocks (they tumble from his hat brim, which is why pieces fall and the next piece waits at the brim).
His showy hands cannot place them true. You can. You never swing a sword.

**Why the order matters.** Huffle's huff blew the Keystones outward in a spiral, nearest first. Each Keystone you set hums one more note of the Sky Lullaby and its
Tether points to the next island. Pirate Cove, flung farthest out to sea, is eleventh. Celestial (10) is where the party meets Huffle and calms her; she points out
to sea to the one Keystone still lost; Pirate (11) is a loud, joyful romp that gathers every ally and the fireworks crew; Carnival (12) is Huffle's own party,
where she hands over Keystone twelve.

**Why it is not an errand quest.** Every chapter has its own villain with a stacking problem, a local ally and a feeling. The Keystone is a payoff, never the
plot of a level. Every level is a small minigame (race, defend, build, destroy, survive, rescue, balance or carry) in its own diorama.

### 2.4 Huffle (the force)

A huge, round, lilac baby cloud in a blanket of cloud, big wet eyes, puffed cheeks. She loves the clatter of toppling blocks and has no one to build with. She hoards
shiny blocks, so she holds Keystone twelve. She appears as a shadow from chapter 1 (a distant huff, a lilac swirl at the edge of each island's sky) and is met in
chapter 10.

## 3. Text, tone and table rules

- **In-level moments are wordless.** No dialogue and no text over the board. Captions are 12 words or fewer on a purple-wood or gold plaque, live UI, never baked in.
- **Names** are 1 to 4 words, at most one pun, and avoid reference-board titles, `minigames.md` naming fixes and all old level names. Not used: Tumble Fair,
  Molten Guardian, Kraken Blockade, Fort Blast, Tumbling Towers, Bridge Bashers, Maze Heist, Cosmic Gateway, Grand Finale, Story Victory, Balloon Race,
  Summit Scramble, Wobble Builders, Bridge Dash.
- **Real tension (user decision)** in Lava and Neon, plus one tension beat each in Ice (ice_08), Underwater (underwater_07) and Clockwork (clockwork_09). Never
  on-screen harm. Every low beat resolves.
- **Beat table columns.** `ID`, `Beat`, `What happens` (exactly 2 sentences: the first is the opening event, the second is the act and payoff), `Objective` (8
  words or fewer, a story goal, no numeral), `Verb` (race, defend, build, destroy, survive, rescue, balance, carry), `Minigame` (the idea that fits the story and
  the 1 or 2 mechanics under it; `module` = one of the 16 in `minigames.md`, `base` = a base pattern from the defence study, `debut` marks the first use),
  `Place` (spot and time of day), `Who`, `Ending` (plain, twist, reveal, guest, secret or story, plus the surprise).
- **Scene notes table** (after each beat table): `opening_event` (a unique id), `footprint` (the board silhouette, never repeated inside a biome), `terrain`,
  `set_piece`, `set_piece_action`, `cast_pose`, `hud_meter` (a meter named in the setting's words). Within a biome 7 or 8 distinct terrains, set pieces and actions,
  none used more than twice, no (terrain, action) pair repeated anywhere in the campaign, at least 4 pose verbs. `react` is for animals and props only.
- **Endings.** At least 4 non-plain per chapter, no pattern more than twice per chapter, at least 4 chapters whose boss ends other than `story`, at most 2 "the scary
  thing was friendly" surprises in the whole campaign (candy_03 and celestial_05), at least one non-plain hard level per chapter, bonuses not always `secret`.
- **Mechanics are phase 3's.** Hooks name an idea and its story reason; the mapping to rules is the systems designer's. Boss rows add no new module (U9).

## Chapter 3: The Quiet Floe (Ice)

**Caption:** A snow knight has frozen the gate. Nobody knows why.
**Where we are and what is wrong.** Frost Harbour, a hush of glacier, jetty and igloos. Sir Shiver, the castle's giant snowman knight, has frozen every path. Keystone three sits above the
gate. Nobody knows why he does it.
**Villain / boss: Sir Shiver.** A towering three-ball snowman knight in a bucket helm. Three phases, beaten by building: snowball volleys (bumper walls), a slide across the rink (stoppers), a
stair up to his helm. The reveal comes at ice_10, not before: he guards the gate "until the Wizard returns", an order the Wizard forgot he gave. Boss triple: arena a long gate rink; 3 phases; action = stoppers
and stairs.
**Ally met: Lana** (alpaca, thread-keeper). She ties the loose Tether threads from Meadow and Candy and knits Ada a scarf that is far too warm.
**Set pieces:** harbour_floe, glass_jetty, lana_igloo, snowball_alley, avalanche_peak, snowman_gallery, frost_lake, castle_gate.
**Emotional beat:** hush; first real care of a hero. **Hat Dip:** a trombone (ice_01). **Debuts (modules):** floe_footing (ice_01), foothold_climb (ice_05).
**Hook into next:** Lana knits everyone woolly bubble hats; the harbour water bubbles and glows from below.
